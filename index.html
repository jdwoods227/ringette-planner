        Learn to Skate (U7/U8 Active Start) – the planner's rules of thumb

        • Fun first. Games, stories and silly names ("pizza stop", "make lemons", "bear, knight, stand"). Every practice ends with a game they'll talk about at home.
        • Minimal lining up. The auto-builder targets under 15% of time in lines: everyone-at-once drills, waves of 4–5 across the width of the ice, and 3–4 short stations (3–4 min each) with a coach at each.
        • High reps, short blocks. 5–8 minutes per activity, 1–2 cues at a time, then let them try. Show, don't tell (under 20 seconds of talking).
        • Teach falling first. Bend knees and sit to the side with chin tucked; get up with bear (hands and knees) → knight (one foot up) → stand. Practise it every session.
        • Skill path: balance & ready position → falling/getting up → marching → two-foot glide → swizzles → snowplow stop → one-foot glide & stride → backward wiggle/swizzles → edges & circle glides → crossover step-overs. Ring and stick work as soon as they can march with confidence.
        • Mixed abilities. Use the regressions (coach tow, hold the boards, march instead of glide) for weak or nervous skaters and the progressions for confident ones – in the same drill.
        • Plan structure (45–50 min): helmet check & warm-up → 2 short skill blocks → water → station rotation → fun game → cool-down huddle.

        Safety & ratios

        • Helmets on, always. CSA-certified helmet with a face mask, chin strap snug (one finger under it), gloves on – for every skater on the ice. Check helmets in the warm-up; re-check after falls.
        • Coach-to-skater ratio. Aim for 1 on-ice coach or helper per 4–6 skaters, and 1 per 3–4 for first-timers or very nervous skaters. Above 1:8, split the group or get more helpers. Enter skaters and coaches on the Plan tab and the planner checks it.
        • On-ice helpers (parents, older players) wear helmets too and must meet your association's screening rules.
        • Space and direction. Everyone skates the same way in waves; keep the gate and boards clear; no sticks above the knees; no pushing or body contact.
        • Watch for cold, tired or upset skaters – a short water break or a coach-helper buddy usually fixes it.

        Ratios, helmet and face-mask rules and helper screening vary by association. Check Ringette Canada's Active Start / learn-to-skate resources and your local association's requirements.

        Learn to Train at U10 – the planner's rules of thumb

        • Fun first, every player active. The auto-builder targets under 20% of time standing in lines (each drill carries an estimate) and favours stations, partner work and small-area games.
        • Lots of touches. One ring per player (or per pair) wherever possible; small groups; short shifts (60–90 s) in games.
        • Play the game to learn the game. Aim for 25–35% of practice in small-area games – cross-ice 3v3 and 2v2 give many more touches than full-ice play.
        • Short instructions. Under 30 seconds on the ice, 2–3 key cues per drill; show it, don't lecture. Use the bench card.
        • Both sides, both directions. Forehand and backhand, left and right crossovers.
        • This is the skill-hungry window. Speed, agility, balance and skating skills develop fast at this age – build them in every practice.
        • Everyone tries goalie. Rotate goaltending in U10 so all players learn the position.
        • Plan structure: warm-up → 2–3 skill blocks → water → small-area game → cool-down & team talk.

        Ringette rules to teach (and the drills that teach them)

        • Blue lines: the ring can't be carried over a blue line – it must be passed across and played by a different player. Blue-Line Pass Relay, Neutral Zone Pass-Through, 4v4 Half-Ice with Blue-Line Rule.
        • Free pass: play starts and restarts with a free pass; the taker has 5 seconds and must pass the ring out of the free-pass circle (not skate it out). Free-Pass Fundamentals, Free-Pass Options, Whistle & Restart Scrimmage.
        • Goal crease: only the goalie may play the ring in the crease; skaters (and their sticks) stay out. The goalie clears it by passing or throwing it out. Crease Rules Game, Goalie Outlet Throw.
        • Three-skater rule: only three skaters per team in an end zone (plus the goalie). Three-in-the-Zone Game.
        • Safety: no body contact; controlled stick checks only – no slashing, hooking or high sticks.

        Rule details and U10 modifications (shot clock, game format, ice size) change between seasons and associations. Check the current Ringette Canada rulebook and Ringette Ontario / your local association's U10 guidelines before teaching them.
